Getting started

Download guava.zip, drag it into Applications and open it. Click Allow when macOS asks about your folders.

Turn on Full Disk Access

Lets the cleanup tools see everything. Takes 30 seconds, once.

  1. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security › Full Disk Access.
  2. Click +, choose guava.zip in Applications, and switch it on.
  3. Quit and reopen guava.zip.

Switched on but scans still say access is missing? Remove it with – and add it again.

Browsing

The sidebar holds Recents, your favourite folders, iCloud Drive and connected drives. Click a column header to sort. The path bar at the top shows where you are; click any part of it to jump there. Select a file to preview it in the Quick Look pane.

Right-click a file to open, rename, duplicate, copy its path or move it to the Trash. Drag files out of guava.zip into any other app.

Search

Type in the search field to filter the folder you're in, or switch the scope to Entire Mac and press Return to search everywhere with Spotlight, including the text inside documents.

AI bar

Press ⌘ K and type what you want in plain English, for example "find the lease PDF from last spring" or "what's eating my disk?". Results appear as rows you can open, reveal or drag.

Before guava.zip moves, renames or trashes anything, it shows you exactly what it will do and waits for you to confirm. It can only work inside your home folder.

Choosing a model (Settings › AI)

  • Claude (cloud). The smartest option. Create an API key at console.anthropic.com and paste it into Settings; guava.zip stores it in your Keychain. Your question, the paths of files it looks at and the text of files it reads are sent to Anthropic. API usage is billed to your Anthropic account.
  • Ollama (local). Private: nothing leaves your Mac. Install Ollama from ollama.com, run ollama pull llama3.1 in Terminal, then choose Ollama in Settings.
  • Off. The bar runs a plain Spotlight search.

To stop the assistant reading what's inside files, turn off "Let the assistant read the text inside files" in Settings.

Cleanup tools

Everything guava.zip removes goes to the Trash, so you can put it back until you empty the Trash.

  • Junk Sweep finds app caches, logs, developer leftovers and used installers in Downloads. Old iPhone backups are listed but left unticked.
  • Big & Forgotten lists your largest files; filter by size and by when you last opened them.
  • Twins finds exact duplicates by comparing contents, and keeps the oldest copy.
  • Space Map shows which folders use the most space; click to drill in.
  • Uninstall Apps removes an app plus the settings, caches and containers it left in your Library.
  • Background Items lists the agents that start when you log in, and removes the ones you installed.

What your Mac will ask you

macOS protects your files and passwords, so it checks with you the first time guava.zip needs something. Here's what each question means and what to click. guava.zip shows this once when you first open it, and it's always in Settings › Permissions (⌘,).

Access to your folders

“guava.zip” would like to access files in your Desktop folder.

You'll see the same question for Documents, Downloads, iCloud Drive, removable volumes and network volumes. guava.zip is a file browser, so it needs to list and tidy the files there. Each folder asks once, the first time you open it.

What to do: click Allow. You can change it later in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Files & Folders.

Your saved API key (Keychain)

guava.zip wants to use your confidential information stored in “zip.guava.app” in your keychain.

This is how guava.zip reads the Anthropic API key you saved in Settings. The key stays in your Keychain, not in a file.

What to do: type your Mac login password and click Always Allow. guava.zip isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so after an update or rebuild your Mac may ask once more.

Data from other apps

“guava.zip” would like to access data from other apps.

Junk Sweep and Uninstall Apps look inside other apps' folders in ~/Library (Containers and Group Containers) to find caches and leftovers.

What to do: click Allow to include those leftovers. Don't Allow just skips them.

Full Disk Access (optional)

Some places, like Mail, Messages and Safari data, are locked even after the prompts above. Without Full Disk Access, Junk Sweep, Space Map and Uninstall Apps skip them and their totals come out a little low. macOS never asks for this; you turn it on yourself. When a scan skips protected folders, guava.zip shows a small banner with a shortcut.

What to do: follow the three steps in Turn on Full Disk Access.

First open of a downloaded copy

“guava.zip” Not Opened. Apple could not verify “guava.zip” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.

guava.zip isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first time you open a copy downloaded from the web, macOS stops it. Copies you build yourself don't get this.

What to do: click Done, open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down to Security, click Open Anyway next to “guava.zip was blocked…”, enter your password, then click Open Anyway again. You only do this once.

Nothing else

guava.zip doesn't control other apps, send notifications, add login items or run in the background, so you won't see Automation or Background Items prompts from it. Moving things to the Trash never asks; it may fail for apps installed for all users, and guava.zip tells you when that happens.

If you see a prompt that isn't listed here, it isn't from guava.zip's features. Don't Allow is always safe.

FAQ

Does guava.zip replace Finder?

It sits alongside it. Finder keeps running (macOS needs it for the desktop), and you use guava.zip whenever you want to find or tidy files.

macOS says it can't verify guava.zip.

Early-access builds aren't notarized yet. See What your Mac will ask you for the one-time Open Anyway step.

Does guava.zip collect data?

No. guava.zip has no accounts and no analytics. The only thing that leaves your Mac is what the AI bar sends to Anthropic when you choose Claude. See the privacy policy.
